Day 1
You'll burn roughly 1,590 to 1,660 calories hiking, about 3,400 to 3,500 calories total for the day. Carry about 2.5 to 3 liters of water — it's frequent for the first 2.3 miles, then there's a dry, steep 2.7-mile stretch (roughly 2.2 hours) before Los Esteros.
Day 2
You'll burn roughly 1,360 to 1,420 calories hiking, about 3,200 to 3,250 calories total for the day. Carry about 4.5 to 5 liters of water, since there's none at all from Los Esteros to Serpent Lake — the entire 6.5-mile day, including the summit, is dry.
Day 3
You'll burn roughly 1,340 to 1,390 calories hiking, about 3,150 to 3,200 calories total for the day. Carry about 5 to 5.5 liters leaving Serpent Lake — the stretch to Indian Lake is 9.1 dry miles, roughly 5.2 hours, so top off there before the final 1.7 miles.

Trail Notes

Navigation & wildlife
Above treeline, the route can become faint — careful navigation is essential in wind, fog, or fresh snow. Elk, deer, marmots, and black bears are all possible in this area; store food and scented items properly overnight. Source: AllTrails – Jicarita Peak

Overnight cold snaps
Clear nights right after rain are the coldest kind at elevation: the humidity and cloud cover that would normally trap some outgoing heat are gone, so radiative cooling can push the overnight low well below what a "typical" clear night would suggest. That compounds with the route's own elevation gain (8,700–12,835 ft), and it's easy for the model above to understate it since it's built on averages. Pack for colder than the displayed low, especially after an afternoon monsoon shower.
Trail status & closures
This route isn't monitored by any weather station, but it is monitored by the trail org: check santafetotaos.org before heading out for active fire closures, trail conditions, and other alerts along the Santa Fe to Taos Trail corridor. As of Sept 2, 2026 the Frijoles Fire closure on the Skyline Trail near the Santa Fe Ski Basin (Winsor Trailhead to south of Lake Katherine, Sections 1–2) is still active and was just extended through Sept 15, 2026 at 8 AM — so it now spans your trip dates, but it still does not overlap this route through Section 4 (Agua Piedra to Jicarita Peak). Conditions change, so it's worth a final check close to the trip.
Frijoles Fire status
Per the September 2, 2026 update from NM Fire Info, the Frijoles Fire is now 59% contained at 15,451 acres, with 519 personnel assigned. It started August 4 from a lightning strike about three miles east of Cundiyo, NM. Recent rain over the fire area has actually slowed firefighter access to some interior pockets; evacuations have been lifted, and a community meeting was held in Truchas the same day. Still worth a fresh check at nmfireinfo.com close to the trip date.
Algae & water safety
Update from Pam Neely, SF2T trail organizer: she checked NMED's current Harmful Algal Bloom advisory map and found no advisory for Los Esteros, Agua Piedra, or anywhere along the SF2T route, and has had zero reports of blooms or algae-related illness from hundreds of SF2T hikers this season. There's no known issue on this route. General background: early September falls within NM's broader HAB season (July–Nov), and small backcountry ponds like these aren't on the state's monitored-lake list, so ordinary visual awareness at any water source is still sensible — blue-green, bright green, brown, or red water, surface scum, or a bad smell are the signs to know. Standard filters and boiling do not remove cyanotoxins, so if a source ever looks suspect, skip it rather than treat it. Illness after exposure: NM Poison Center, 1-800-222-1222.
