Trail Notes

Getting down: plans A, B & C
Plan A — ride the tram down. The route ends at the tram's upper terminal, and hike-up, ride-down is a standard option the tram explicitly supports, with one-way tickets (about $24 at last check). Sandia Peak Tramway confirmed by phone on Oct 3 that you can buy one-way tickets down at the top, last minute, on Friday — no need to buy online ahead of time, despite the Balloon Fiesta crowds. Getting people off the mountain is a priority for them, but there may be a wait of a few hours for a car down. The tram also runs weather permitting; high winds can shut it down. Tickets & hours at sandiapeak.com.

Plan B — hike back down La Luz. The full out-and-back this page plans for; the start time is set so it finishes before sunset (see the Down card at the top for how much margin that leaves).

Plan C — hike down to the 10K Trailhead for a pickup. If the tram falls through and nobody's up for the whole descent, take the King of the Mountain Trail to the 10K Trail (South) and down to the 10K Trailhead parking lot on NM 536, where you can get a ride if you need one. Those trails are open, and the 10K north lot is fully open with restrooms (as of Oct 3), so a driver can park there and wait.
Water & sun
There's no reliable water anywhere on La Luz — carry everything you'll need from the start (see Energy & Hydration above). The lower half climbs through open, south- and west-facing desert scrub with very little shade, and you'll be coming back down through it in the late afternoon, when the lower switchbacks can be far warmer than the forecast at the top suggests. UV peaks around midday, while you're on the upper climb (and getting the most of the altitude boost shown in the UV chart), and the cool air up there makes it easy to forget — a sun hoodie and visor, sunscreen, and sunglasses. The upper terminal and the restaurant at the top are possible places to buy a drink, but don't count on them.
Terrain & pace
La Luz is well-marked and easy to follow, but long stretches are loose decomposed granite and, higher up, rocky talus slopes with uneven footing. That makes the descent slower than the simple pace model on this page assumes — budget extra time coming down, and use poles. Stay on the switchbacks rather than cutting corners. The last stretch follows the crest south from the top of La Luz to the tram terminal, mostly gently rolling at 10,300–10,600 ft.
Cold, wind & altitude at the top
The route rises about 4,100 ft from the parking lot (6,517 ft) to the high point (10,646 ft) near the top of La Luz — about 4,350 ft of total climbing once the ups and downs along the way are counted. Expect the crest to be noticeably colder and windier than the trailhead, and October can bring the first snow or ice to the shaded upper switchbacks — if a storm passes through in the days before, check recent AllTrails reviews for conditions. Altitude can also make the upper climb feel harder than the numbers suggest, especially for anyone coming from low elevation.
Daylight
On Oct 9, sunrise is around 7:07 AM and sunset around 6:38 PM in Albuquerque. If the day runs long, the descent is the worst place to lose the light — bring a headlamp.
Wildlife
Rattlesnakes are possible on the warm, rocky lower trail, even in early October on a sunny day — watch where you put your hands and feet, and skip the headphones so you'll hear a rattle. Ankle-high boots and long trekking pants help: a hiker who steps near a snake is most exposed at the foot, ankle, and lower leg, and even a layer of fabric between fang and skin substantially cuts the venom delivered. Trekking poles help too — planted ahead of you, their vibration tends to warn snakes off, and they keep a bit of distance between you and anything coiled beside the trail. If someone is bitten, per the CDC: call 911, keep calm and still, remove rings or watches before swelling starts, and don't cut, suck, ice, or tourniquet the bite. Black bears live in the Sandias too; don't leave food unattended at the top.
Trail status & closures
La Luz is in the Sandia Mountain Wilderness, managed by the Sandia Ranger District of Cibola National Forest. The Sandia Crest area itself is closed for a Forest Service project through Jan 15, 2027 — including the Crest Spur from La Luz to the Crest House and NM 536 beyond the 10K Trailhead — but La Luz up to the tram, King of the Mountain, and the 10K Trail (South) are open, so the route and all three plans for getting down are unaffected. Check Cibola NF alerts before heading out for any fire restrictions, closures, or trail damage, and check sandiapeak.com for the tram's status the morning of.
